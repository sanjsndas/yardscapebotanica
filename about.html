Our Story, Rooted in Passion

From a single crimson rose to a movement of red-themed abundance — meet the heart & soil behind YardScape Botanica.

❤️ The Seed of YardScape Botanica

Founded in 2015 by master horticulturist Elena Vasquez, YardScape Botanica began as a tiny urban garden in Denver, Colorado. What started with 12 red rose bushes and a dream has blossomed into one of the most trusted resources for red-themed gardening, ecological design, and vibrant landscapes across North America.

Our mission is simple: transform outdoor spaces into living art using the power of red — whether it’s a balcony drenched in scarlet geraniums or a five-acre edible forest with ruby chard and pomegranates. We believe gardening should be joyful, regenerative, and bold. The color red symbolizes life’s intensity: love, courage, and the fiery energy of growth.

Today, YardScape Botanica serves over 120,000 monthly gardeners through our guides, workshops, and custom design consultations. We work with homeowners, schools, and botanical gardens to create resilient habitats that buzz with pollinators and dazzle the eye. Our team holds degrees in plant science, landscape architecture, and soil ecology — but more importantly, we all share a deep-rooted love for getting our hands dirty.

🌱 Our Gardening Philosophy

At YardScape Botanica, we embrace "Regenerative Red" — a holistic approach where aesthetics meet ecology. We avoid harsh chemicals, prioritize heirloom and native red plants, and design landscapes that support birds, butterflies, and soil microorganisms. Every red petal we plant serves a purpose: feeding a bee, cooling a microclimate, or telling a story of passion.

🌍 Beyond The Garden: Ethical Roots

YardScape Botanica is a certified B Corp candidate (in progress) and a proud member of the Sustainable Gardening Alliance. We partner only with nurseries that follow organic practices, and we offset 100% of our shipping carbon via tree-planting initiatives — guess which tree we love planting? Acer rubrum ‘Red Sunset’, of course. Additionally, 5% of annual profits are donated to urban gardening programs in food deserts, helping communities grow their own fresh produce (with plenty of scarlet peppers and ruby tomatoes).

Our "Seeds for Good" program has donated over 50,000 red seed packets to schools and community gardens since 2020, promoting pollinator awareness and nutritional security. We're also reducing plastic: all our mailed guides use recycled kraft paper, and we encourage digital resources. When you learn with us, you become part of a movement that's rooted in respect for the Earth and each other.
